The test of understanding is explaining it simply to someone who was not there. Your friend knows school algebra and has seen the puzzle's forty primes, and thinks that forty in a row must mean a real pattern.

## Where this leads

From here on, every result in the curriculum is either proved, or stated with the reason it is not.

<Foreshadow lesson="M1.9">

Some claims about every whole number are easiest to prove one step at a time. M1.9's induction proves the first case, and proves that each case gives the next, so that the chain reaches every whole number, as forty checks never could.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M3.2">

A limit is defined with "for every" and "there is": for every tolerance, there is a distance that keeps the function within it. Its proofs are direct proofs that find the distance for any tolerance, and that a function has only one limit at a point is proved by contradiction.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M9.7">

A hypothesis test reasons like a proof by contradiction: assume the model is no better than chance, and show that results this good would then be rare. Rare is not impossible, so the test never reaches a true contradiction, and M9.7 shows what it can conclude instead.

</Foreshadow>
