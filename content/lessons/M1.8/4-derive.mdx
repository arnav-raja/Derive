## Root three

The same argument shows that $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational, with "a multiple of 3" in place of "even". An integer $n$ is a *multiple of 3* when $n = 3k$ for some integer $k$. Dividing an integer by 3 leaves just one remainder, 0, 1 or 2, so a number of the form $3m + 1$, which leaves 1, is not a multiple of 3. The argument needs one claim of its own: if $n^2$ is a multiple of 3, then $n$ is a multiple of 3. Prove it by the contrapositive. An integer that is not a multiple of 3 leaves a remainder of 1 or 2 when divided by 3, so it is $3k + 1$ or $3k + 2$ for some integer $k$: two cases, which between them cover it. In the first, $n^2 = 9k^2 + 6k + 1 = 3(3k^2 + 2k) + 1$, which is not a multiple of 3. Now the second case: suppose that $n = 3k + 2$.

So $n^2$ leaves a remainder of 1 in this case too, and is not a multiple of 3. Both cases give the contrapositive, and so the claim. From there the proof for $\sqrt{2}$ goes through word for word, with 3 in place of 2: $p^2 = 3q^2$, so $p = 3r$, so $q^2 = 3r^2$, so $p$ and $q$ are both multiples of 3, against lowest terms.

It must fail for $\sqrt{4}$, which is 2, a fraction, and it does: there the step would need "if $p^2$ is a multiple of 4, then $p$ is a multiple of 4", which $p = 2$ breaks. Finding where a proof fails for a claim that is false is a good test of whether you understand it.

## Where to stop looking for factors

To test whether 999,983 is prime, a program can try each whole number from 2 upwards. It need not try them all, because a whole number of at least 2 that is not prime always has a small factor. The proof needs one fact about inequalities: two inequalities between positive numbers can be multiplied, so if $a > c$ and $b > d$, with $c$ and $d$ positive, then $ab > cd$. The reason: multiplying $a > c$ by the positive $b$ gives $ab > cb$, and multiplying $b > d$ by the positive $c$ gives $cb > cd$, so $ab > cd$. Without positive numbers it can fail: $-1 > -2$, but $(-1) \times (-1) = 1$ is less than $(-2) \times (-2) = 4$.

So a search for a factor of a whole number $n$ of at least 2 can stop at $\sqrt{n}$: if no whole number from 2 to $\sqrt{n}$ divides $n$, then none does apart from 1 and $n$, and $n$ is prime. For 999,983, that is 998 divisions instead of nearly a million. The Build phase's test for primes relies on exactly this proof.

It also explains the puzzle. For $n$ from 0 to 39, the value of $n^2 + n + 41$ is less than $41^2 = 1{,}681$, so a value that was not prime would have a factor from 2 to 40, and its smallest such factor would be a prime below 41, which the Explore phase showed never happens. At $n = 40$ the value reaches $41^2$ itself, and the run ends.
