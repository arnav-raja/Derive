## Where this leads

Every claim in the rest of the curriculum is built from these words: not, and, or, if-then, for every and there is.

<Foreshadow lesson="M1.8">

A proof is a chain of steps that leaves no case uncovered. M1.8 proves claims directly, by the contrapositive, as Derive's chain allows, and by contradiction: assume the claim's denial, worked out with this lesson's rules, and reach something impossible.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M4.2">

Probability measures sets of outcomes. Events are sets, "A or B" is their union, and this lesson's count of a union becomes the probability of a union: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M3.2">

A limit is defined with two quantifiers in a fixed order: for every tolerance, there is a point beyond which the values stay within it. As the grid of models and learning rates showed, swapping the two would change what the definition says.

</Foreshadow>
