This lesson builds on school arithmetic and on the powers of ten from M1.1. Four quick questions check that both are ready. Answer from memory, without a calculator. A miss is useful: it opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Exercise id="recall-order" />

<Reteach for="recall-order">

Multiplication comes before addition unless brackets say otherwise: $4 \times 2 = 8$ first, then $3 + 8 = 11$. Working strictly from left to right gives 14, which is the value of a different expression, $(3 + 4) \times 2$. The Explore phase shows what this convention really decides: the shape of the expression.

</Reteach>

<Reteach for="recall-power-quotient">

<Callback lesson="M1.1">

Dividing powers of ten subtracts their exponents: $10^5 \div 10^2 = 10^{5-2} = 10^3 = 1{,}000$. Five tens multiplied together, divided by two of them, leaves three.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-negatives" />

<Reteach for="recall-negatives">

A negative times a negative is positive, so $(-3) \times (-4) = 12$. You probably learnt this as a rule to remember. In the Derive phase you will prove it from a rule you already trust, so it never needs memorising again.

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-distribute" />

<Reteach for="recall-distribute">

Split 98 as $100 - 2$ and multiply each part by 7:

$$
7 \times 98 = 7 \times (100 - 2) = 7 \times 100 - 7 \times 2 = 700 - 14 = 686.
$$

Multiplying out a bracket like this is the distributive law, one of the rules this lesson is about.

</Reteach>
