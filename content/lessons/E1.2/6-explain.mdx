## Where this leads

From here on, nearly every program chooses and repeats, and an invariant is how you can be sure of what it does.

<Foreshadow lesson="E1.3">

A loop and its tests often make up one job, such as counting the halvings down to any floor. E1.3 gives such a job a name of its own, a function, so that it is written once and used anywhere: a floor goes in, a count comes out.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M4.1">

A loop that runs a random experiment a million times and counts how often something happens estimates its probability. M4.1 does exactly that, then checks the counts against probabilities worked out exactly.

</Foreshadow>

Binary search halves the stretch it is searching on every pass, as the puzzle halved the learning rate, and an invariant, that what it seeks is still inside the stretch, proves that it finds it.
