Everything that a call of `step` needs from the calls before it, the best loss so far and the count since it, lives on the object, so two watchers kept for two runs never mix their counts. PyTorch's `ReduceLROnPlateau`, which lowers the learning rate once the loss stops improving, keeps a best loss, a patience and a count of bad epochs as `EarlyStop` does, and is called once an epoch, as `step` was here, though it acts only once the count is more than the patience, and counts a loss as a new best only when it beats the best by a small margin.

## A sum that changes its inputs

<Exercise id="debug-vec-add" />

</Reveal>

<Reveal for="debug-vec-add">

An operator that makes a new value should leave its inputs alone, as `+` on numbers does, since code such as `w = u + v` goes on to use `u`. Changing a value in place has operators of its own: `u += v`, which on numbers is short for `u = u + v`, calls `u.__iadd__(v)` when the class has that method, which may change `u` in place, and otherwise works out `u + v` and makes `u` refer to the new object. PyTorch marks its methods that change a tensor in place, such as `add_`, with an underscore at the end.
