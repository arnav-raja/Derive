This lesson builds on M1.1's units, M1.2's algebra, M1.3's functions and M1.4's rates of change. Five quick questions check that they are ready. Answer from memory, without running anything. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Exercise id="recall-turns" />

<Reteach for="recall-turns">

<Callback lesson="M1.1">

A unit can be multiplied by a ratio that equals 1, such as $\frac{1 \text{ minute}}{60 \text{ s}}$, and cancelled like a letter in algebra. So $7{,}200 \frac{\text{turns}}{\text{minute}} \times \frac{1 \text{ minute}}{60 \text{ s}} = 120 \frac{\text{turns}}{\text{s}}$. The same move turns degrees into radians in this lesson.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-square" />

<Reteach for="recall-square">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

$(x - a)^2$ is $(x - a)(x - a)$. Multiplying each term of the first bracket by each term of the second gives $x^2 - ax - ax + a^2 = x^2 - 2ax + a^2$. The middle term is easy to lose: try $x = 3$ and $a = 1$, where $(3 - 1)^2 = 4$ and $9 - 6 + 1 = 4$, while $9 - 1 = 8$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-inverse" />

<Reteach for="recall-inverse">

<Callback lesson="M1.3">

A function has an inverse only if no two inputs share an output. Squaring sends $3$ and $-3$ to the same $9$, so on all numbers it cannot be undone: given 9, nothing says which input it came from. Narrowing the inputs to the numbers that are 0 or more leaves one input for each output, and there $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ undoes squaring. This lesson narrows its own functions the same way.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-composition" />

<Reteach for="recall-composition">

<Callback lesson="M1.3">

$g(f(x))$ works from the inside out: first $f$, then $g$ on what $f$ gave. With $f(x) = x + 3$ and $g(x) = 2x$, $f(1) = 4$ and then $g(4) = 8$. Doing $g$ first would give $f(g(1)) = f(2) = 5$, a different number, so the order matters.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-rate" />

<Reteach for="recall-rate">

<Callback lesson="M1.4">

The average rate of change of $f$ between $u$ and $v$ is the change in output over the change in input, $\frac{f(v) - f(u)}{v - u}$, the slope of the chord joining the two points of the graph. For $f(x) = x^2$ between 1 and 3 it is $\frac{9 - 1}{3 - 1} = 4$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>
