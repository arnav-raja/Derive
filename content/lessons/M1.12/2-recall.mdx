This lesson builds on M1.2's algebra and its rules for powers, M1.4's quadratics and M1.11's turns. Five quick questions check that they are ready. Answer from memory, without running anything. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

To multiply out two brackets, multiply each term of the first by each term of the second and add the four products: $(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd$. Check with numbers: $(2 + 3)(4 + 5) = 5 \times 9 = 45$, and $8 + 10 + 12 + 15 = 45$. This lesson multiplies out brackets in which one of the terms is a new kind of number.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-no-real" />

<Reteach for="recall-no-real">

<Callback lesson="M1.4">

The solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$, and $b^2 - 4ac$ decides how many there are: two when it is positive, one when it is 0, and none among the real numbers when it is negative, since no real number has a negative square. For $x^2 + 2x + 3 = 0$, $b^2 - 4ac = 4 - 12 = -8$, and completing the square shows why there is no solution: $x^2 + 2x + 3 = (x + 1)^2 + 2$, which is never less than 2.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-quarter-turn" />

<Reteach for="recall-quarter-turn">

<Callback lesson="M1.11">

Turning the plane anticlockwise through $\theta$ about the origin takes $(x, y)$ to $(x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta,\; x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta)$. A quarter turn has $\cos\theta = 0$ and $\sin\theta = 1$, so it takes $(x, y)$ to $(-y, x)$: $(1, 0)$ goes up to $(0, 1)$, and $(0, 1)$ goes over to $(-1, 0)$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-exponent" />

<Reteach for="recall-exponent">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

$b^m$ is $m$ copies of $b$ multiplied together, so $b^m \times b^n$ is $m + n$ copies: $b^m b^n = b^{m + n}$, and $10^2 \times 10^3 = 100 \times 1{,}000 = 10^5$. M1.5 kept this rule when it gave powers a meaning for every number. This lesson gives a meaning to a power whose exponent is a new kind of number, and the rule still holds.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-angle-point" />

<Reteach for="recall-angle-point">

<Callback lesson="M1.11">

Turning $(1, 0)$ anticlockwise through $\theta$ along the circle of radius 1 about the origin reaches $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. At $60°$ the point, the origin and $(1, 0)$ make a triangle with three equal sides, so the point sits straight above $x = \frac{1}{2}$, at height $\sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The point at $30°$ is its mirror image in the line $y = x$, with the two coordinates swapped.

</Callback>

</Reteach>
