The test of understanding is explaining it simply to someone who was not there. Your friend worked through E1.1 and E1.2 with you, so names, values, conditions and loops are familiar, but they have never written a function of their own.

## Where this leads

From here on, every program is built from functions, and every function is judged by its contract.

<Foreshadow lesson="E1.4">

Numbers, strings and bools cannot be changed in place. A call can give its caller a new one, by returning it or, with `global`, by assigning a main-program name, but it cannot alter the one the caller already holds. E1.4's lists can be changed in place, so a function given a list can change the very list its caller holds.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="E1.5">

Each call has names of its own, so when a function calls itself, every call in progress keeps its own values. E1.5 uses that to solve a problem by solving smaller copies of it.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="L1.1">

A model is a function with parameters, such as a straight line with its slope and its intercept, and learning is choosing the parameters that fit the data. L1.1 fits one, first by hand and then by rule.

</Foreshadow>
