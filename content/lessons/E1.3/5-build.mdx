The last test swapped `lr_at` and `clip` for stand-ins and checked that `update` followed them. Only a test does that: in the program each of those names is assigned once, by its `def`, so `update` is still pure. That is abstraction seen from the other side: `update` relies only on what `lr_at` and `clip` promise, so either of them can be rewritten, or replaced by a better one, without touching `update`. Libraries for training models are built this way, which is why a schedule can be swapped for another with a change to one line.
