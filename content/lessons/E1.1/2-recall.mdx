This lesson builds on M1.1's powers of ten and on M1.2's trees and rounded calculator. Four quick questions check that they are ready. Answer from memory, without a calculator. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Reteach for="recall-scientific">

<Callback lesson="M1.1">

In `2.5e-3`, the `e` means "times ten to the power", so it is $2.5 \times 10^{-3}$. A negative power of ten divides: $10^{-3} = \frac{1}{1{,}000} = 0.001$. So $2.5 \times 10^{-3} = 0.0025$: the point moves three places to the left.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-tree-root" />

<Reteach for="recall-tree-root">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

The order of operations fixes the tree: powers first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. In $2 + 3 \times 4^2$ the power is worked out first, $4^2 = 16$, then the multiplication, $3 \times 16 = 48$, and the addition last, $2 + 48 = 50$. The operation done last joins everything else, so it sits at the top of the tree.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-left-chain" />

<Reteach for="recall-left-chain">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

A chain of additions and subtractions is worked from the left, so $20 - 6 - 4$ means $(20 - 6) - 4 = 14 - 4 = 10$. Grouping from the right, $20 - (6 - 4) = 18$, is a different tree with a different value, which is why the convention matters.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-rounded-sum" />

<Reteach for="recall-rounded-sum">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

The calculator rounds after every operation. $1.00 + 0.004 = 1.004$, which rounds to 1.00, so the first 0.004 is lost; adding the second one is lost the same way, and the display shows 1.00. Near 1 the calculator's steps are 0.01 wide, and anything smaller than half a step disappears. Grouped the other way, $1.00 + (0.004 + 0.004) = 1.008$, which rounds to 1.01: with rounding, the grouping can change the answer.

</Callback>

</Reteach>
