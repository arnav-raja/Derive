The test of understanding is explaining it simply to someone who was not there. Write for a friend who is clever but has never heard of a FLOP and has never seen units cancel.

## Where this leads

The four ideas from this lesson return throughout Derive, most of all wherever a model meets real hardware.

<Foreshadow lesson="L6.10">

Planning a training run asks this lesson's puzzle at full scale. The compute comes from $6ND$, it is divided by what a whole cluster sustains rather than one chip, and the time is turned into days and then into money. The unit bookkeeping is exactly what you did here.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="E6.3">

The roofline model divides the FLOP a program does by the bytes it moves, giving FLOP per byte. Comparing that with the chip's own ratio, its FLOP per second divided by its bytes per second, says whether a program is held back by arithmetic or by memory, before you time anything.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="E7.1">

The arithmetic of large models is unit bookkeeping with powers of ten: bytes per parameter, FLOP per token, seconds per step and chips per job.

</Foreshadow>
