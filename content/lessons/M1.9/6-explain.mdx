The test of understanding is explaining it simply to someone who was not there. Your friend has seen the puzzle, and thinks that the only way to be sure is to have a computer try every one of the 1,048,576 places for the cut square.

## Where this leads

<Foreshadow lesson="M1.10">

Counting arrangements leads to formulas for every $n$: $n$ things can be put in order in $n! = 1 \times 2 \times \dots \times n$ ways, and $(a + b)^n$ multiplies out by the binomial theorem. M1.10 proves both by induction on $n$.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M5.1">

The errors of a method that improves its answer step by step form a sequence defined by a recurrence. Induction proves that they shrink, one step at a time, as $d_t \le 0.9^t d_0$ did, and M5.1 asks what a sequence shrinks towards, and whether it settles at all.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="E1.10">

Merge sort sorts a list by sorting its two halves and merging them. E1.10 proves that it sorts by strong induction on the length, as `power` was proved right, and counts its work with a recurrence that this lesson's methods solve.

</Foreshadow>
