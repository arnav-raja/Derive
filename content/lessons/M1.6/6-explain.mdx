## Where this leads

Sums and products of long runs of terms are the everyday language of the rest of the curriculum.

<Foreshadow lesson="M1.9">

Pairing and subtracting found this lesson's formulas, and checking cases suggested them. Induction proves a formula for every $n$ at once: show it for the first case, then show that each case carries the next one with it.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M3.8">

The area under a curve is approached by sums of ever more, ever thinner strips, each sum written with $\Sigma$. The number those sums close in on is the integral, and it is found much as this lesson found the total of a sum with no last term.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M4.5">

The average outcome of a sum of random quantities is the sum of their average outcomes, whether or not they depend on one another. It is the rule for splitting a $\Sigma$, carried over to chance, and it makes hard averages easy.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="L6.3">

An agent's *return* adds its rewards with weights $1, \gamma, \gamma^2, \dots$, where $\gamma$, the Greek g, is the discount this lesson called $r$. Because the weights form a geometric series, the return stays finite even for a task that never ends.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M9.5">

Momentum is the running average of gradients from Derive, with weights on past gradients that shrink geometrically. How quickly they shrink decides how long the optimiser remembers, roughly $\frac{1}{1 - \beta}$ steps.

</Foreshadow>
