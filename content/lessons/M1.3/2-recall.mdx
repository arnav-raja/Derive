This lesson builds on M1.2's way of solving equations, one step that can be undone at a time, and on working out an expression from its tree. Four quick questions check that both are ready. Answer from memory, without a calculator. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

Work up the tree from the leaves. The square comes first, and it applies to the whole of $-3$: $(-3)^2 = 9$. Then $2 \times 9 = 18$, and last $18 - 1 = 17$.

Put 25 in place of $C$ and follow the order of operations: $1.8 \times 25 = 45$, then $45 + 32 = 77$. So 25 °C is 77 °F, a warm day.

</Reteach>

<Reteach for="recall-squares">

$(-5)^2$ means $(-5) \times (-5)$, and a negative times a negative is positive, so it is 25, the same as $5^2$. That is why squaring both sides of an equation, in the lesson on algebra, could add a solution: squaring forgets the sign.
