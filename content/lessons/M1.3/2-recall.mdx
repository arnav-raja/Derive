This lesson builds on M1.2's way of solving equations, one step that can be undone at a time, and on working out an expression from its tree. Four quick questions check that both are ready. Answer from memory, without a calculator. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Reteach for="recall-solve">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

Undo what was done to $x$, last step first. Subtract 2 from both sides to get $3x = 15$, then divide both sides by 3 to get $x = 5$. Both steps can be undone, so no solution is lost or gained. Check it: $3 \times 5 + 2 = 17$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-evaluate" />

<Reteach for="recall-evaluate">

Work up the tree from the leaves. The square comes first, and it applies to the whole of $-3$: $(-3)^2 = 9$. Then $2 \times 9 = 18$, and last $18 - 1 = 17$.

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-convert" />

<Reteach for="recall-convert">

Put 25 in place of $C$ and follow the order of operations: $1.8 \times 25 = 45$, then $45 + 32 = 77$. So 25 °C is 77 °F, a warm day.

</Reteach>

<Exercise id="recall-squares" />

<Reteach for="recall-squares">

Both $4^2$ and $(-4)^2$ are 16, because a negative times a negative is positive. That is why squaring both sides of an equation in M1.2 could add a solution: squaring forgets the sign.

</Reteach>
