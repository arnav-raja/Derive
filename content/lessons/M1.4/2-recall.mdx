This lesson builds on M1.1's units, M1.2's algebra and M1.3's functions. Four quick questions check that all three are ready. Answer from memory, without a calculator. A miss opens a short re-teach straight away.

<Reteach for="recall-expand">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. The middle term, $2ab$, is the part that is easy to forget: each of the two copies of $a + b$ multiplies each term of the other, and $ab$ turns up twice. With $a = x$ and $b = 3$ this gives $x^2 + 6x + 9$. Check it at $x = 1$: $(1 + 3)^2 = 16$, and $1 + 6 + 9 = 16$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Reteach for="recall-solve">

<Callback lesson="M1.2">

Gather the $x$ terms on one side with steps that can be undone. Subtract $2x$ from both sides to get $3x - 3 = 9$, add 3 to get $3x = 12$, and divide by 3 to get $x = 4$. Check it: both sides are 17 when $x = 4$.

</Callback>

</Reteach>

<Callback lesson="M1.3">

$f(5)$ is the output of $f$ when the input is 5. Put 5 wherever $x$ appears: $5^2 - 4 \times 5 = 25 - 20 = 5$.

<Callback lesson="M1.1">

Speed is distance divided by time, and the units divide as well: $150 \text{ km} \div 2 \text{ h} = 75 \text{ km/h}$, read "75 kilometres per hour". Each hour, the train covers another 75 km.
