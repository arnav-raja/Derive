The test of understanding is explaining it simply to someone who was not there. Your friend knows school algebra, has seen the puzzle's three waiting times, and has never heard of completing the square.

## Where this leads

Lines, rates and bowls run through the rest of Derive.

<Foreshadow lesson="M3.1">

The average rate of change is measured between two points. Shrinking the gap between them to nothing gives the rate at a single point, the derivative. For a quadratic $px^2 + qx + r$ you have already met it: the rate between two inputs depends only on their midpoint, so at a single point $x$ it is $2px + q$.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="M3.6">

Training a model means making its *loss*, a score of its misses such as the squared error, as small as possible. Near its lowest point, almost any smooth loss looks like a bowl. Gradient descent finds the bottom by taking small steps downhill, which works even when there is no formula to complete the square on.

</Foreshadow>

<Foreshadow lesson="L1.2">

Fitting a straight line to data by least squares is the puzzle with two numbers to choose instead of one: the squared error is a bowl in both, and its bottom is the best line.

</Foreshadow>
